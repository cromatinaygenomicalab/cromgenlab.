Lines I and II · Basic research

Who deposits H3.3, and where?

The histone variant H3.3 is deposited into chromatin by two routes: DAXX together with ATRX, and HIRA. CromGenLab studies what happens to gene regulation when this machinery is altered in colon cancer, glioblastoma and prostate cancer.

Ongoing projects

Histone chaperones and gene regulation

  1. H3.3 incorporation as a mechanism of gene regulation by DAXX and its isoforms, in constitutive heterochromatin and in DAXX-regulated promoters.
  2. Role of ATRX and DAXX in the regulation of the genes IFITM3 and MT2A.
  3. The HIRA chaperone as an antagonist at DAXX-regulated genes.

Loss-of-function models in glioblastoma and colon cancer cell lines: siRNA against DAXX and ATRX, and CRISPR-Cas9 and Cas13 targeting DAXX.

Environment

Epigenetics, diet and aging

In collaboration with other groups, the lab studies how aging, a high-fat diet and environmental enrichment change gene regulation and memory in mouse models, using RNA-seq and methylome (RRBS) data from the dorsal hippocampus. It also studies the effect of PM2.5 and PM10 particulate matter on the ATRX/DAXX complex in lung adenocarcinoma cells.
