Chromatin Regulation and Genomics Laboratory

CromGenLab

From chromatin regulation to precision medicine.

Instituto Nacional de Cancerología · Mexico City

We study how the genome is packaged, what happens when that packaging fails in cancer, and how to turn that knowledge into biomarkers for Mexican patients.

We integrate basic research, clinical data, omics technologies and computational analysis. Our work ranges from histone chaperones in cell lines to a cohort of thousands of patients with testicular germ cell tumors.

In brief

Frequently asked questions

What is CromGenLab?

CromGenLab is the Chromatin Regulation and Genomics Laboratory at the Instituto Nacional de Cancerología (INCan), Mexico’s National Cancer Institute. It studies how chromatin is organized, which epigenetic and genomic alterations arise in cancer, and how to turn them into biomarkers for precision medicine.

Where is it and who leads it?

The laboratory is part of the Basic Research Division of INCan in Tlalpan, Mexico City. It is led by Dr. Rodrigo González Barrios de la Parra, group leader, with Dr. Miguel Santibáñez Andrade as associate investigator.

What does it study in testicular cancer?

Through TESTINCan, a platform of more than 4,000 patients with testicular germ cell tumors (TGCT) treated at INCan between 2007 and 2025, it searches for genetic, epigenetic and imaging biomarkers of risk, prognosis and response to cisplatin.

Which technologies does it use?

Whole-exome sequencing (WES), aCGH, DNA methylation with Illumina EPIC v2, ATAC-seq, RNA-seq and ChIP-seq, together with CT radiomics, computer vision and deep learning for multimodal data integration.

En español. CromGenLab es el Laboratorio de Regulación de la Cromatina y Genómica del Instituto Nacional de Cancerología (INCan), en la Ciudad de México, dirigido por el Dr. Rodrigo González Barrios de la Parra. Estudia la regulación de la cromatina, las variantes y chaperonas de histonas (DAXX, ATRX, HIRA, H3.3) y la epigenética y genómica del cáncer, y desarrolla biomarcadores para tumores de células germinales de testículo a través de TESTINCan, una plataforma de más de 4,000 pacientes.
